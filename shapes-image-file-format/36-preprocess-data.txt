36 — does boosting colour separation before encoding improve the cut?

Idea supplied by the project owner: the dog photograph from report 33, run through a heavy
contrast/saturation boost, then put through the report 35 pipeline unchanged.

Source: a 1170x855 JPEG, resampled to 738x539 so the width matches report 33's original.
Encoded mark: 1,009 regions / 34.25 dB. WebP arm: cwebp -m 6 -q 12 = 5,562 B at 34.63 dB.
Our file at that mark: 8,908 B (walls 6,754, colour 2,130, header 24).

    runs/35-bgclass.sh with IMG=dogpop738 REGIONS=1009

=== THE CUT IS MUCH BETTER, ON BOTH ARMS ===

                        ORIGINAL dog        PRE-PROCESSED dog
arms agree                    94.50%                   98.86%
region blobs                      77                       22
pixel  blobs                     451                       63
region dE on edge               5.47                    10.88
pixel  dE on edge               4.04                     9.82

Every number improves, and the two arms nearly stop disagreeing (98.86%).

=== BUT THE FORMAT'S ADVANTAGE SHRINKS ===

blobs, region vs pixel     5.9x                 2.9x
dE,    region vs pixel     1.35x                1.11x

At radius 7 the pixel arm is AHEAD on edge fidelity (9.54 vs 9.38).

MECHANISM: the region graph's advantage was largely that it regularises an error-prone
classifier. Make the classifier reliable and there is less left to regularise. Cleanly separated
colour classes are exactly what makes a per-pixel decision reliable.

=== AND IT INTRODUCES A COLLISION THE ORIGINAL DID NOT HAVE ===

Probed colours from the pre-processed frame:

  background sky/trees   (330,100) (480,100) (189,63) (568,63)  ->  rgb(0,0,0)
  dog's nose/eyes        (460,214)                              ->  rgb(0,0,0)

EXACTLY equal. The boost clipped both to pure black.

In the ORIGINAL the same pair was separable in principle: the dog's ear was rgb(42,44,39) and the
foliage rgb(10,16,16) to rgb(54,60,60) — close, overlapping, but not identical. Clipping made the
collision total and unrecoverable. It is visible in 36-preprocess/dogpop-cut.png as holes in the
dog's face where the eyes, nose and mouth were.

So the boost TRADED one collision for another:
  gained   grass separates cleanly. Grass blue channel falls to 8-58 while the dog's blue tracks
           its green at 100-142, so the blue channel alone now discriminates.
  lost     every dark dog feature and the whole dark background became the same pure black.

=== WHAT TO DO INSTEAD ===

A NON-CLIPPING transform. Boost chroma separation while preserving luminance detail — rotate or
stretch hue, raise saturation, but do not crush the endpoints. The gain here came from the
chromatic axis (blue channel); the loss came from clipping the luminance axis. They are
independent, so the gain is available without the loss. UNTESTED — stated as the next experiment,
not as a result.

=== CAVEATS ===

  - One image, one preprocessing setting, applied by hand outside this pipeline. The exact
    transform is unknown to the code: an already-processed JPEG was supplied.
  - Different crop and size from report 33's original (738x539 vs 738x414), so the two are not
    pixel-comparable; the comparison is between summary statistics, not between matched runs.
  - Sample coordinates were re-picked for this frame. The FIRST attempt put two "keep" points on
    grass — caught by reading the printed rgb values, which is why the probe step exists.
  - Bytes: 8,908 B against WebP's 5,562 B at matched fidelity, i.e. +60.2%. Unchanged story, and
    not what this report is about.
