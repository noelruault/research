39 — connectivity post-pass: fill enclosed background, drop what is not attached to the subject.

Owner's observation: "stuff not connected to the centered object is usually background and can be
discarded." It is two distinct operations that fix two different failures.

  1. HOLE FILLING. A background component that never touches the image border is enclosed by the
     subject, so it is almost certainly a misclassified part of it. Flip it to keep.
     Fixes: the bobcat's dark markings, the dog's eyes.
  2. DISCONNECTED REJECTION. Of what remains kept, keep only the component containing the subject
     seed (centroid of the keep examples). Anything not attached to the subject is background
     however much it looks like it.
     Fixes: the dog's floating tree blobs.

APPLIED TO BOTH ARMS with identical code. Falsification #14 was giving one arm a cleanup the other
did not get. Disable with CONN=0 to recover the raw classifier output.

    lab bgclass <source> <ours> <rival> <out> keep=... remove=...
    CONN=0 lab bgclass ...        # rollback to raw masks

FOREGROUND COLLAPSES TO EXACTLY ONE PIECE on both images and both arms. Background blobs fall from
133 to 10 (bobcat) and 44 to 4 (dog) on the region arm. The dog's removed-pixel count rises by
11,412 — that is the detached tree blobs being discarded, which is the operation working.

=== IT WORKS, AND IT DOES NOT CLOSE THE GAP TO THE PIXEL ARM ===

After the pass, region vs pixel blob counts are 11 vs 9 (bobcat) and 5 vs 6 (dog). Essentially
equal. Connectivity is a graph operation available on ANY substrate; it is not a format advantage.
The format's advantage here remains what report 35 (corrected) established: cost (140-249x fewer
decisions) and edge fidelity, not fragmentation.

On the REGION arm both operations are available on the region adjacency graph at O(regions)
instead of O(pixels). They are computed on the pixel mask here so both arms run exactly the same
computation and the mask comparison stays fair. The cost claim is separate and is not measured by
this run.

=== THE LIMIT, VISIBLE IN BOTH IMAGES ===

Connectivity cannot reject background that TOUCHES the subject.

  dog     — the detached tree blobs on the right are gone. The tree mass directly behind the
            dog's ear SURVIVES, because it is one connected component with the dog.
  bobcat  — the branch crossing in front of the cat SURVIVES for the same reason.

So the rule is: disconnected background is free to remove; connected background is untouched. On
these two photographs that removes most but not all of the error, and what remains is exactly the
achromatic collision at the point of contact.

=== CAVEATS ===

  - Two images. The seed is the centroid of the keep examples; if that centroid lands on a removed
    pixel the code falls back to the largest kept component, which is a heuristic and untested.
  - "Background must touch the border" fails for a subject that is cropped at the frame edge, and
    for a background fully enclosed by the subject (a doughnut). Neither occurs here.
  - Keeping only the seed component discards legitimately detached subject parts — a thrown ball,
    a second animal, an arm behind an occluder. It is a strong assumption stated as a heuristic,
    not a general rule.
  - No ground-truth masks, so these are still fragmentation counts, not accuracy. Consultant item
    S0 (report 38) is what fixes that.
