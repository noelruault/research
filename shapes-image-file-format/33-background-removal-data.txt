33 — background removal: region graph vs pixel grid, and matched-fidelity bytes.

Source: six consumer background-removal photographs (JPEG), decoded once with `sips -s format png`
and used identically by every arm. 256x197 to 738x414.

Commands (from code/lab, under bash):

    for f in $SRC/*.png; do lab hd "$f" $OUT/$(basename $f .png); done
    lab p4enc  $OUT/<img>/hd_<mark>.png $OUT/<img>.shpc
    lab psnr   $SRC/<img>.png $OUT/<img>/hd_<mark>.png
    cwebp -quiet -m 6 -q <q> $SRC/<img>.png -o t.webp ; dwebp -quiet t.webp -o t.png
    lab bgcut  $SRC/<img>.png $OUT/<img>/hd_<mark>.png <img>-webp.png out.png <tol>
    lab sbs    $SRC/<img>.png $OUT/<img>/hd_<mark>.png <img>-webp.png sbs.png

TWO CAVEATS, BOTH AGAINST US:
  - images-4 and images-5: WebP hit its quality FLOOR at q1 and overshot our fidelity by
    1.30 dB and 0.26 dB. It would be smaller still if it could match exactly, so the stated
    delta UNDERSTATES WebP's advantage on those two.
  - Sources are JPEG. Every arm inherits the same prior loss; this is not a clean-source test.

=== 2. BACKGROUND REMOVAL: same flood, two substrates ===

Algorithm, identical on both arms: seed from the image border, absorb a neighbour whose colour is
within `tol` of the seed set's running mean. Arm R traverses the region graph of our partition
(1,229 regions); arm P traverses the pixel grid of WebP's decoded output at matched fidelity.
Same seeds, same tolerance, same acceptance test.

dog photograph, 738x414 = 305,532 px. Background is visually ~85% of the frame.

tol   arm  steps      selected            sel px    frayed edge
 28   R      1571      265/1229 regions   151519              0
 28   P    382120      -                   96106          15832
 55   R      3032      633/1229 regions   232656              0
 55   P    731252      -                  183389           6633
 90   R      4251      990/1229 regions   267142              0
 90   P    885296      -                  221900           4672

Steps ratio: 241x (tol 55) in favour of the region graph. Masks agree on 61.55% / 81.48% /
83.85% of pixels at tol 28 / 55 / 90.

WHAT THE REGION GRAPH WINS: 241x fewer flood decisions, and the selection is a list of 633 ids
rather than 232,656 pixels. That is the O(regions) claim, measured.

WHAT "FRAYED EDGE" IS AND IS NOT: it counts mask-edge pixels whose neighbour across the edge is
in the SAME region of our partition, i.e. the mask cut through a region interior. Arm R's zero is
DEFINITIONAL, not evidence -- its mask edges ARE region boundaries. Arm P's figure is judged by
our partition, which is a biased referee. Read it as "the pixel wand disagrees with the image's
own tonal structure this many times", not as a neutral quality score.

WHAT NEITHER ARM WINS: the actual job.
  tol 28 -- background largely SURVIVES. Grass is texture: many regions of drifting colour.
  tol 55 -- background mostly gone, but THE DOG IS BEING EATEN (belly and legs selected).
  tol 90 -- almost everything selected.
There is no setting in between. A white dog in sunlight and bright sunlit grass are not separable
by colour distance, and moving from pixels to regions changes the UNIT the failing test is applied
to, not the test. See 33-bgcut/dog-bgcut-tol55.png: the cutout keeps the tree line, drops parts of
the dog, and leaves grass fragments.

In the fidelity panel the dog's outline IS traced by region boundaries -- which is why the mask
mechanics work -- while the grass is shattered into texture regions, which is why the selection
does not.

Caveats.
  - Six images, one subject type: a centred subject on a natural background.
  - The flood is a deliberately simple rule. A better selector on the SAME region graph might do
    far better; this tests the substrate, not the ceiling. The substrate being 241x cheaper is
    what would make a smarter selector affordable.
  - No comparison against a real background-removal tool (rembg, SAM). Those solve the semantic
    half shown here to be missing, and that is the honest comparison to run next.
