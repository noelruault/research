A1 — does the colour-driven merge dissolve a sprite's silhouette?

Commands (from code/lab):

    go build ./...
    SP=/Users/noelruault/go/src/github.com/noelruault/sprites/assets/props/extra
    for s in ak74 bow pickaxe; do
      go run . hd $SP/$s.png $OUT/$s
      for f in $OUT/$s/hd_*.png; do
        go run . silhouette $SP/$s.png $f $OUT/vis-$s-$(basename $f .png).png 8
      done
    done

Method. The merge renders every region as one flat colour, so a region straddling the
silhouette shows the SAME colour on both sides of it. For every adjacent pixel pair crossing
the true alpha silhouette (one a=0, one a>0), the test is whether the render's colour changes
there. No change => both pixels are in one region => the silhouette was dissolved at that
crossing. Needs no label dump.

"invisible" counts crossings where the value load() hands the merge on the OPAQUE side is
already near-black (all premultiplied channels <= 24), and is therefore indistinguishable from
the void. These are crossings no merge rule could ever separate, because the information was
destroyed before the merge ran.

THE RELATION THAT MATTERS: dissolved <= invisible on every row, all three sprites, every mark.

  pickaxe: dissolved 15, invisible 15 -- EXACTLY equal. Every dissolved crossing is one the
           merge was handed as black-on-black. Zero crossings dissolved that the merge could see.
  bow:     invisible 99.28%, and dissolved plateaus at 259 across three successive marks
           (476, 804, 1368 regions). A floor, not a merge effect -- at 1368 regions on a
           3420 px image the merge is barely merging anything and 37% is still gone.
  ak74:    invisible 76.07%; dissolved rises 36.75% -> 62.39% as regions coarsen, never
           exceeding invisible.

Mechanism, confirmed by the panel images in A1-silhouette/: load() at common.go:32 discards
alpha (`r, g, bl, _ := src.At(...).RGBA()`), and Go's PNG decoder returns PREMULTIPLIED values.
So a fully transparent pixel arrives as (0,0,0) = black, and a soft rim pixel arrives darkened
toward black in proportion to its transparency. A dark sprite on a transparent background
becomes a dark shape on a black background. The two are the same colour, and no merge
constraint can separate colours that are equal.

Visible in ak74-328regions.png: the orange wooden stock's silhouette SURVIVES (orange != black)
while every edge of the black gun body, grip, muzzle and barrel is marked dissolved. Same
image, same merge, opposite outcomes, decided purely by whether the edge colour differs from
black. In bow-476regions.png the bowstring is dissolved along its entire length.

WHAT THIS RETRACTS. PREREGISTRATION.md Phase 1 item 2 and DESIGN-ALPHA.md proposed the fix
"treat an alpha difference as an infinite merge cost, so regions never merge across a
transparency edge". That fix is aimed at the wrong stage. The merge is behaving correctly given
its input; it never joined two crossings it could distinguish. Adding the constraint would
paper over an upstream loss.

THE ACTUAL FIX: stop destroying alpha at load. Carry it into the merge. Once the merge can see
alpha, a transparency edge IS a difference, and the constraint is either trivially satisfied or
unnecessary -- which A1 cannot yet distinguish, because no version of the merge has ever
received an alpha channel.

Caveats.
  - Three sprites, all small (400 to 3420 px). Same corpus limitation as the A3 pilot.
  - The blackish threshold is 24/255, picked to catch premultiplied soft-rim pixels, not tuned.
    A different threshold moves the "invisible" column but cannot move the dissolved column,
    which is measured from the render and is threshold-free.
  - Fully transparent pixels are treated as void and any a>0 as object, so the silhouette here
    is the outermost edge of anything drawn at all.
  - This measures dissolution of the silhouette only. It says nothing about whether the merge's
    regions are good, and nothing about colour fidelity.
