35 — supervised colour classification of regions vs pixels.

Same corpus and same arms as report 33. The ONLY change is the selection rule: report 33 flooded
from the image border with a drifting RGB tolerance; here the caller points at examples and every
unit is classified by 1-nearest-neighbour in CIELAB. Same classifier, same examples, both arms.

    lab bgclass <source.png> <ours-render.png> <rival.png> <out.png> keep=x,y;.. remove=x,y;..

Examples are sampled from the SOURCE (what a user would be pointing at). Ties go to keep, so the
subject is never lost on a tie. CIELAB rather than RGB because a shadowed white hair and a sunlit
blade of grass are close in RGB magnitude and far apart perceptually — the exact confusion that
sank report 33's flood.

THE TREND IS THE FINDING. Sharpening the classifier from 8 to 18 examples removed 45,081 more
background pixels (the tree line started going) and:
    per-region blobs   75 -> 77    (flat)
    per-pixel blobs   263 -> 451   (+71%)
A sharper decision boundary fragments a per-pixel mask and does not fragment a per-region one.
The partition regularises the decision spatially for free; there is nothing to regularise with
morphology afterwards.

=== WHAT WORKS, AND WHAT DOES NOT ===

WORKS: chromatic separation. Green grass against a white/black dog is cleanly separable once
examples are supplied. Report 33's failure was the ALGORITHM (a drifting unsupervised flood), not
the representation. Mask agreement between arms rises from 61.55% under the flood to 91.96-95.40%
under supervision, i.e. both substrates now largely agree because the rule is actually right.

DOES NOT WORK: achromatic collision. The dog's black ear is rgb(42,44,39) and the dark foliage
behind it is rgb(10,16,16) to rgb(54,60,60). The bobcat's dark markings sit inside the range of
its own blurred dark background. These are the SAME COLOUR to any colour rule, so no set of
colour examples separates them, on either substrate. Visible in the images: the dog cut keeps
part of the tree line; the bobcat cut has holes in the animal's dark markings.

That residue is precisely what a learned model buys and colour cannot: iOS Lift Subject uses
shape and semantics, not a colour lookup. This report does not claim to match it and did not test
against it.

=== CAVEATS ===

  - Two images, one subject type (a centred animal on a natural background).
  - Examples were chosen by hand, by the author, looking at the picture. A real interaction
    supplies one touch point, not seven; how the result degrades with fewer/worse examples is
    untested.
  - "Blobs" counts 4-connected components of each mask value. It measures fragmentation, which is
    a proxy for how much morphological cleanup a mask needs. It is not a ground-truth accuracy
    measure, and there is no ground-truth mask for these images.
  - No comparison against rembg, SAM or Lift Subject. Those solve the semantic half.
  - The per-region arm classifies OUR partition's region colours; the per-pixel arm classifies
    WebP's decoded pixels at matched fidelity. Both are downstream of a lossy encode.

EDGE FIDELITY — the surviving claim, measured on a NEUTRAL referee.
Report 33's "frayed edge" was scored against our own partition, which is a biased referee. This
scores against the SOURCE image: for every mask-edge pixel pair, the CIELAB step across it. A mask
sitting on genuine image edges scores high; one cutting through flat areas scores low.

  region arm is 28-54% better at EVERY filter setting, on BOTH images:
    dog     5.47/4.04=1.35   4.60/3.27=1.41   4.12/3.05=1.35   3.82/2.99=1.28
    bobcat 11.51/7.47=1.54   9.86/6.67=1.48   8.35/5.79=1.44   7.23/5.37=1.35

  and it uses ~30% FEWER edge pixels to do it (dog 9,001 vs 13,811; bobcat 9,850 vs 13,610), so
  the boundary is shorter as well as more decisive.

  The filter DEGRADES edge fidelity on both arms: it buys smoothness by cutting through real
  edges. That is a trade a region mask does not have to make.

DECISIONS RATIO is untouched (140-249x) and the filter sharpens rather than blunts it: the pixel
arm NEEDS the cleanup to compete on fragmentation (451 -> 89), the region arm barely moves
(77 -> 52) because there was little to clean. The pixel path costs 305,532 classifications PLUS a
7x7 majority pass; the region path costs 1,229 classifications and no pass.

FURTHER CAVEAT ON THE SURVIVING CLAIM: edge fidelity is a proxy too. A mask edge on a large colour
step is evidence it follows real structure, not proof it follows the RIGHT structure. A
confidently wrong boundary also scores well.

THE DEEPER TEST STILL NOT RUN: give the pixel arm its own partition — SLIC or Felzenszwalb
superpixels over WebP's output, classified the same way. That isolates "our segmentation" from
"having any segmentation at all", and it is the honest apples-to-apples. It needs a segmenter this
environment does not have installed.
